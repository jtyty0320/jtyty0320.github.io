Soft Sensors

Optical fiber tactile sensors, force myography sensors, and self-sensing actuators

I develop soft sensors that are thin and flexible enough to be worn on the body or mounted on a robot. Most of them use light guided in optical fibers as the sensing signal. Pressing or bending the fiber changes how much light reaches the detector, which makes the sensors immune to electromagnetic interference.

Optical fiber fingertip tactile sensors

PofTac is a plastic optical fiber-based tactile sensor that measures contact location, force magnitude, and force distribution over the fingertip. It is built to work the same way on a human fingertip and on a robot fingertip (Chung et al., 2026; Chung & Kyung, 2026).

Optical fiber force myography (FMG) sensors

Force myography reads hand motion and grip from the small pressure changes that forearm muscles produce on the skin. I designed thin and flexible FMG sensors made of weaved optical fiber, in which light loss through controlled fiber-polymer contact encodes the applied force. The work started from a proof-of-concept sensor (Chung et al., 2024) and a sleeve-type system (Chung et al., 2024), and was then optimized for higher sensitivity and robustness (Chung et al., 2025; Chung et al., 2025; Chung et al., 2025).

Self-sensing soft actuators

An electrohydraulic actuator can also act as its own sensor. Using a capacitance sensing circuit, I made an EPIC actuator that detects touch and estimates force without an external sensor (Chung et al., 2022).
